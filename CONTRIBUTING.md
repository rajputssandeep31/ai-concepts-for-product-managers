# Developing and reviewing lessons

## A lesson is ready for editorial review when

- Its prerequisites have already been introduced.
- It defines the concept in plain language without hiding essential distinctions.
- It includes one concrete example and explains the PM decision affected.
- It identifies a misconception and offers an exercise with a reasoned answer.
- Its technical claims link to primary sources opened during research.
- Its examples are public-safe, hypothetical where appropriate, and contain no invented customer outcomes.
- Its previous/next links and index entry resolve.

## How to evolve the guide

Develop lessons sequentially after the opening sequence is reviewed. Keep future topics in the roadmap until substantive articles exist. Add corrections to the changelog, with the affected lesson and why the correction matters. Recheck the relevant sources before describing changed vendor or protocol behavior.

Use diagrams when they explain an interface or flow more clearly than prose. Use tables for comparisons. Do not add model rankings, unverified benchmark claims, transient pricing or generic AI news simply to make the repository look active.

Record an implementation example as tested only when it has actually been run. A conceptual diagram, fictional request or proposed experiment must be labelled accordingly. Do not turn a hypothetical result into a personal achievement.
